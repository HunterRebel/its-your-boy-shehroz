I
Written August 30

There are people we know every day, people we lose touch with, and then there are those strange few who somehow remain somewhere in the quiet corners of our memory without even trying.

You're probably one of those people.

It's funny, isn't it? We haven't really talked in about a year. Maybe by now, you barely remember the conversations we had, and honestly, I can't claim that I know the person you are today either. We've both lived an entire year of things the other person knows nothing about. New days, new thoughts, new people, new disappointments, new little victories … and somewhere in between all of that, we became almost strangers again.

And yet, I remembered your birthday.

IV

I don't know why some dates stay in your mind while hundreds of others disappear. Maybe not every person is meant to remain close; some people simply cross our lives for a short distance, leave behind a small memory, and continue walking in a direction we may never know.

So today, I'm not going to write one of those usual birthday wishes about "success, happiness and endless blessings" and disappear after sending it.

(if you're wondering why a stranger is writing you a letter this earnest — I don't fully know either. Some feelings just insist on being written down.)

V
what I actually hope for you
  • I genuinely hope this next year of your life is kinder to you than the last one.
  • I hope you become someone your younger self would look at with a quiet sense of pride.
  • I hope the things you never tell anyone eventually become lighter.
  • I hope the dreams you're too afraid to speak about find their way toward you.
  • I hope you meet people who understand the parts of you that you usually keep hidden — not because you explain them perfectly, but because they simply notice.

And if this year brings you a few difficult nights, I hope you remember that a difficult chapter is still only a chapter.

VI
while I'm being honest

You are the kind of
guy people remember.

Not because you tried to be. Not because you worked at an impression. Because of something that just is — the specific way you show up, the way you move through a room, the way things are a little more interesting for you being in them. Birthdays are just the world's excuse to say what it already knows about you. Today it's saying it out loud.

VII
a few things that are just true
01
You change the room, quietly
You don't announce yourself when you walk in, but the room is usually different after you've been in it. That's not an act. It's just what happens when someone is actually paying attention.
02
You don't do things halfway
When you commit to something, you commit to it fully. Not everyone can honestly say that about themselves. You can.
03
You hold things together
On the days things fall apart, you're usually the reason people keep going. That's not a mood you're in every once in a while. It's a habit you've built without asking for credit.
04
You say what's real
No performance, no softening it past recognition. People know where they stand with you. That's rarer than it should be, and it matters more than you probably think.
05
You're playing the long game
You're not chasing quick wins. You're building something that's still going to matter in ten years, even on the days nobody's clapping for it.
06
You don't leave
The people who matter to you know they can count on that. You don't disappear when things get complicated. That kind of loyalty is worth more than most people ever say out loud.
07
You notice things first
You read rooms, situations, people, before anyone else catches up. You probably don't think of it as a skill. It is one.

None of that needs proving to me. I hope this year gives you every reason to believe it about yourself, too. Be strong on the days that ask for it, and be gentle with yourself on the days that ask for that instead — you're allowed both. Be the man you're already quietly becoming. You don't have to earn the rest of what's coming for you.

VIII
notes gathered along the way
on distance
Distance doesn't erase a person. It just puts them in a drawer instead of on the desk. Still there. Still yours whenever you open it.
on the year you didn't post about
The parts of the year nobody saw are usually the parts that mattered most. Nobody has to see it for it to have counted.
on being tired
Tired isn't the same as failing. Sometimes it just means you've been carrying something for longer than anyone realized, including you.
on starting over
Starting over isn't going back to zero. You keep everything you learned the first time. That's not nothing — that's the whole advantage.
on the people who left
Some people were only ever meant to walk beside you for a season. That doesn't undo what the season gave you.
on forgiving yourself
You were doing the best you could with what you knew at the time. That sentence is allowed to apply to you too.
on plans that changed
The version of this year you planned in January and the version you actually lived are rarely the same. Usually the second one taught you more.
on the quiet wins
Getting up on the hard mornings. Answering the phone when you didn't want to. Staying, when leaving would've been easier. Those count. Nobody hands out medals for them, but they count.
on the version nobody's met yet
There's a version of you a year from now who knows things this year's you hasn't learned yet. You're already on your way to becoming them.
on today
Today doesn't need to be a big day to matter. It just needs to be yours. That's already enough of a reason to mark it.
on comparing years
This year doesn't have to beat last year to count. Some years are for building quietly underground, where nothing shows yet.
on the apology you never got
You're allowed to stop waiting for it and heal anyway. Closure is something you're also allowed to give yourself.
on small rooms
Not every year needs an audience. Some of the most important growing happens in rooms nobody else ever walks into.
on the thing you almost said
If it's still sitting in your chest a year later, it probably still wants saying. There's no expiry date on being honest.
on becoming someone new
You don't owe anyone the version of you they met first. People are allowed to keep meeting you, again and again.
on the ordinary tuesdays
Nobody writes letters about the ordinary tuesdays, but they're usually where a life is actually being lived.

X

Maybe we'll talk again someday. Maybe we won't.

Maybe this message will become just another notification in your inbox, something you read once and forget. And that's completely okay.

Because not every good wish needs a long history behind it. Sometimes, a person can simply wish another person well — even from a distance, even after becoming strangers, even without knowing what life looks like on the other side of the screen.

So, on 30th August —

XI
Happy
Birthday.

Not just "happy birthday" because it's your birthday, but genuinely … I hope life gives you reasons to be grateful for the person you're becoming.

XII
a few things I wish for you
  • May you have the courage to leave what no longer belongs in your life, the patience to wait for what does, and the wisdom to know the difference.
  • May your failures teach you without defining you.
  • May your successes humble you without changing you.
  • May the people you choose bring peace rather than confusion.
  • May you never have to beg for a place in anyone's life.
  • And may you never become so busy surviving life that you forget to actually live it.

XIV

I don't know where you are in life right now. I don't know what you're fighting, what you're building, what you're hoping for, or what you've quietly given up on.

But wherever you are —

  • I hope you're okay.
  • I hope you're growing.
  • I hope you're finding your way.

And perhaps the strangest part of this whole message is that we're almost strangers writing memories into each other's lives without even realizing it.

Still … 30th August deserves a little pause.

XV
a few things worth remembering
It's okay to rest before you're finished. Rest isn't the opposite of progress.
It's okay to not have the answer yet. Some questions take years, not evenings.
It's okay to ask for help. Needing someone isn't the same as failing on your own.
It's okay to change your mind, even about things you were once certain of.
It's okay to outgrow people, plans, and versions of yourself that used to fit.
It's okay to say it plainly when something is heavy, instead of dressing it up so it's easier for other people to hear.
It's okay to want good things for yourself, without immediately explaining why you deserve them.
It's okay to let this year be different from the last one. It already is, just by arriving.

P.S.

And one more thing, since I'm already this far in — you don't owe anyone a version of yourself that has it all figured out. If there's ever a night when things feel heavier than they should, you don't have to carry it alone, and you don't have to explain it perfectly for it to be worth saying out loud. Whatever you're chasing, whatever you quietly think you don't deserve yet — you do. You always did. Don't be afraid of where life is taking you. Be curious about it instead.
